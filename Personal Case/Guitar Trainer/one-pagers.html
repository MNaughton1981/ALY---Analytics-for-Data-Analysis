★ This Week's Session — start here tonight

Week 1: C & G major on the D-G-B string set. 30 minutes, whatever days you can.

MinDo
0–5Warm up: play a C major triad arpeggio slowly, then a C major scale. Relaxed hands.
5–23Core: the three C major inversions on D-G-B (see sheet 1), then the G–C–D loop (sheet 2). Loop the hardest change, don't restart.
23–28Play the G–C–D–C loop in time at 60 bpm. Sing the top note: D–E–D–E.

Three checkpoints for the week

  • I can play all three C major inversions on D-G-B cleanly, no buzzing.
  • I can name the top note of each shape without looking.
  • I can loop G–C–D–C choosing the nearest shape each time.

If you only get one session

  • Do the G–C–D–C loop with nearest inversions while singing the top note. Highest-leverage 30 minutes.

The one habit — "call your shot"

  • One beat before every change: look at where the next shape lives, say its top note out loud, hum it — then land and check.
  • Say it → region-to-name. Hum it → name-to-sound. Play it → both confirmed. Just the top note is enough.

1 · C Major on D-G-B

One chord, three inversions, same three notes (C-E-G) — different note on top.

Do this

  • Play each shape slowly; say "C major, [inversion], top note [note]" out loud.
  • Climb: 1st inversion -> 2nd -> root, then back down, staying on D-G-B.
  • Once clean, play just the top two strings — that's your future melody voice.

2 · G–C–D in One Neighborhood (D-G-B)

Voice-leading drill: change chords by moving the shortest distance. Top voice = D–E–D–E.

ChordFrets (D-G-B)InversionTop note
G5-4-3RootD
C5-5-5SecondE
D4-2-3FirstD

Do this

  • Loop G–C–D–C at 60 bpm: four beats each, then two, then one.
  • Keep your hand close to the strings; look at the destination shape before you move.
  • Articulation: try short stabs on beats 2 & 4, then slide into a shape from one fret below.

3 · C Major on A-D-G

The same three inversions, one string set lower — closer to the bass.

Do this

  • Play all three inversions up and back down the A-D-G set.
  • Bass-awareness: add the open A or low E only if the note belongs to the chord.
  • Compare each shape to its D-G-B cousin — notice how the neck connects (sheet 6).

4 · C Major on G-B-E

Highest chord tone lands on the first string — best set for chord melody and fills.

Do this

  • Play each inversion and sing the top string note — this is your melody voice.
  • Strum only strings 3-2-1; let the top note ring longer than the others.
  • Move between this set and D-G-B for the same chord to hear the register change.

5 · Minor Triads (C minor)

Major becomes minor by lowering ONLY the third one fret. Watch the orange dot drop.

On D-G-B
On A-D-G

Do this

  • Major↔minor switch: play C then Cm, changing only the third. Repeat with G/Gm, D/Dm.
  • Flip the label toggle to Intervals and watch the 3 become b3.
  • Minor i-iv-v: Am–Dm–Em using the nearest inversions.

6 · Connecting the Inversions Up the Neck

The plateau-breaker: the same C major chord lives in three neck zones on one string set.

C major — three inversions on D-G-B
C major — three inversions on A-D-G

Do this

  • Play 1st -> 2nd -> root -> 2nd -> 1st inversion, moving smoothly up and back down.
  • Name the top note as you land on each shape.
  • Woodward calls this a "triad scale" — try running it while a metronome ticks slowly.

7 · Chord Melody with Triads

Put the melody note on TOP. This four-chord line has top notes E–F–G–G on the B string.

BeatChordTop noteFrets (D-G-B)
1CE5-5-5
2FF7-5-6
3GG9-7-8

Do this

  • Play the four shapes in a slow loop; make the top note louder than the inner notes.
  • Then play ONLY the top line (E-F-G-G) as a melody, then add the chords back.
  • Remember: not every melody note needs a full chord under it.
