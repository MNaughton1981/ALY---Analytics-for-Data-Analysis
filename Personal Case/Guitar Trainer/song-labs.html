1 · The Weight Key of A

Trains: major triads + your first minor (iii) triad in a progression — I–iii–IV, with V in the turnaround.

Verse: A   C#m   D   A   ·   Turnaround leans on E (V)
ChordRoleD-G-B fretsInversionTop note
AI2-2-22ndC#
C#miii2-1-21stC#
DIV4-2-31stD
EV2-1-0RootB

Do this

  • Loop A–C#m–D–A at 60 bpm. Notice A and C#m share the same top note (C#) — only the inside voice moves. That is the whole lesson of this song.
  • Say it out loud: "one – three-minor – four." Hear how the C#m darkens the middle of the phrase.
  • Add the E (V) as a two-beat pickup back to A. Sing the top line C#–C#–D–B.

2 · Dead Flowers Key of D

Trains: I–V–IV country-rock changes and crisp articulation — stabs, slides, and rests.

Core changes: D   A   G   D   (I–V–IV–I)
ChordRoleD-G-B fretsInversionTop note
DI4-2-31stD
AV2-2-22ndC#
GIV5-4-3RootD

Do this

  • Play the three shapes as short stabs on beats 2 & 4, muting between — that is the country-rock lift.
  • Then slide into each shape from one fret below. Keep the hand relaxed; let the slide do the work.
  • Top voice barely moves (D–C#–D). Let it ring while the lower voices get the rhythmic chop.

3 · Friend of the Devil Key of G

Trains: spotting and playing a minor (ii) triad among majors, and the I–IV move.

Verse: G → C (I–IV, over a descending G scale)  ·  Bridge introduces Am (ii)
ChordRoleD-G-B fretsInversionTop note
GI5-4-3RootD
CIV5-5-52ndE
Amii2-2-12ndC

Do this

  • Loop G–C first (I–IV). Top voice climbs D→E — hum it before you play it.
  • Now drop in Am: flip the label toggle to Intervals and watch the 3 turn into b3. That b3 is why it sounds sad.
  • Compare Am to C: they share the notes C and E — only one voice changes. Find the common tones before moving.

4 · Into the Mystic Key of C

Trains: legato I–V–IV changes where common tones ring across the chord move.

Core: C   G   C   (I–V–I)  ·  lift to F (IV) in the refrain
ChordRoleD-G-B fretsInversionTop note
CI5-5-52ndE
GV5-4-3RootD
FIV7-5-61stF

Do this

  • Play C–G as smoothly as possible: hold pressure, move the shortest distance, let notes overlap (legato).
  • The top line C→G is E–D–E–F when you add the F — a gentle rising melody. Keep it singing on top.
  • Try the whole loop with clean tone and no pick attack on the top string — just let it bloom.

5 · Franklin's Tower Key of A (Mixolydian)

Trains: triad soloing over a two-chord vamp — improvise using ONLY the current triad's tones.

Vamp: A ↔ G (I–bVII)  ·  D (IV) lands the "roll away the dew" turn
ChordRoleD-G-B fretsInversionTop note
AI2-2-22ndC#
GbVII5-4-3RootD
DIV4-2-31stD

Do this

  • Set a slow A–G vamp (use the metronome/backing loop in practice-tools).
  • Over A, play only A–C#–E; the instant it turns to G, switch to G–B–D. Nothing else. This is the discipline.
  • Once that is automatic, connect the two shapes with a single passing note, then resolve onto D for the turn.

6 · Ripple Key of G

Trains: chord melody / melody-on-top — this is the workbook's G–C–D drill applied to a song.

Core: G   C   G   D   (I–IV–I–V) — same shapes as Module 2's drill
ChordRoleD-G-B fretsInversionTop note
GI5-4-3RootD
CIV5-5-52ndE
DV4-2-31stD

Do this

  • You already know these grips from the workbook drill — now make the top voice sing (D–E–D) louder than the inner notes.
  • Play only the top line as a melody, then add the chords back underneath it.
  • Remember: not every melody note needs a full triad. Alternate full chords, the top two strings, and single notes.
