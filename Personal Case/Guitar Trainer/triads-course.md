# Triads Across the Fretboard

*A practical, printable guitar course built around voice leading, minor harmony, chord melody, and classic song play-alongs.*

**Prepared for Matt Naughton**
**Standard tuning: E A D G B E**

---

## Course Purpose

Turn familiar chord progressions into a fretboard map. The goal is not to memorize isolated diagrams. It is to see major and minor triads, choose the nearest inversion, hear the top-line melody, and connect those shapes to scales you already partly know.

### How to use this book

- Practice with clean tone and a metronome before adding gain or effects.
- Say the chord name, inversion, and top note aloud.
- Use the recording as a timing and listening reference, but use licensed sheet music for complete, exact arrangements.
- Do not rush into every string set at once. Begin with D-G-B, then add A-D-G.

### Copyright note

This workbook contains original exercises and short chord-cycle references. It does not reproduce lyrics, signature riffs, melodies, solos, or full copyrighted arrangements.

### Four triad qualities

| Quality | Formula | C example | Sound/function |
|---|---|---|---|
| Major | 1 3 5 | C E G | Stable, bright |
| Minor | 1 b3 5 | C Eb G | Darker, settled |
| Diminished | 1 b3 b5 | C Eb Gb | Tense, leading |
| Augmented | 1 3 #5 | C E G# | Unsettled, expanding |

**Core idea:** An inversion does not change the chord name. It changes which chord tone is lowest: root position has 1 in the bass, first inversion has 3 in the bass, and second inversion has 5 in the bass.

## Module 1: Reading the Shapes

### Fingering conventions

- 1 = index, 2 = middle, 3 = ring, 4 = little finger.
- Fret numbers are absolute. Move every note by the same number of frets to transpose.
- Suggested fingering is a starting point. Adjust for hand size and the phrase that comes next.
- Mute unused strings with the fretting hand and picking hand.

## Module 2: Major Triads on D-G-B

### Movable shape formulas

| Inversion | Low-to-high chord tones | Relative fret pattern | C example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Root | 1-3-5 | x, x-1, x-2 | 10-9-8 |
| First | 3-5-1 | x, x-2, x-1 | 14-12-13 |
| Second | 5-1-3 | x, x, x | 5-5-5 |

The first-inversion C shape at 14-12-13 is fully movable; the open-position 2-0-1 version is equivalent but not movable.

### Key drill: G, C, and D in one neighborhood

| Chord | D-G-B frets | Inversion | Top note |
|---|---|---|---|
| G | 5-4-3 | Root | G |
| C | 5-5-5 | Second | E |
| D | 4-2-3 | First | D |

**Practice:** Loop G-C-D-C at 60 bpm. Hold each chord for four beats. Then two beats. Then one beat. Keep the hand close to the strings and listen to the top voice: G-E-D-E.

### Major articulation menu

- Short stabs on beats 2 and 4.
- Arpeggiate low-middle-high, then high-middle-low.
- Slide into the shape from one fret below.
- Hammer one chord tone into place while the other two remain held.
- Play only the top two notes once the full shape is clean.

## Version 2 Expansion: CAGED Connections

Use CAGED as a landmark, not another memorization project.

| CAGED form | Compact triad to locate | Practical question |
|---|---|---|
| C form | A-D-G root-position area | Where are 1-3-5 inside the full grip? |
| A form | D-G-B second-inversion area | Can I remove the doubled notes? |
| G form | G-B-E root-position area | Which top note defines the fill? |
| E form | D-G-B root-position area | Can I slide into the compact upper chord? |
| D form | G-B-E second-inversion area | Where is the nearest melody-on-top voicing? |

- Form the full CAGED chord, then release every duplicated root or fifth until only three different notes remain.
- Name the root, third, and fifth inside the shape.
- Move to the nearest inversion of the same chord without changing string set.
- Play a scale fragment around the triad, always returning to a chord tone.

## Version 2 Expansion: Jerry Garcia Triad Studies

These are original learning routines inspired by chord-tone awareness and melodic voice leading. They are not transcriptions of recorded solos.

### Ripple: melody and harmony
- Play the progression with one compact inversion per chord.
- On a second pass, play only the highest note of each voicing.
- On a third pass, connect those top notes with one neighboring major-scale note.

### Friend of the Devil: major/minor awareness
- Mark every minor chord in your licensed chart.
- Play only root-third intervals first so the major/minor quality is unmistakable.
- Restore the fifth after the transitions are clean.

### Brokedown Palace: expressive chord melody
- Find phrase-ending melody notes by ear.
- Choose inversions that place those notes on top.
- Let long notes sustain and use single-note approaches between triads.

### Franklin's Tower: chord-tone targeting
- Comp through the repeating cycle with triads.
- Improvise one measure using only the current triad notes.
- Add one scale passing tone, but land on the next triad when the harmony changes.

## Version 2 Expansion: Minor Scale Roadmap

Build outward from what you already know.

| Stage | Material | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A minor pentatonic | Name A, C, and E everywhere in the box. |
| 2 | Add B and F | Turn the box into A natural minor. |
| 3 | Relate to C major | Hear the same notes with C as home. |
| 4 | Add G# selectively | Hear E major or E7 resolving to Am. |
| 5 | Transpose to E minor | Repeat the process in a guitar-friendly key. |

### Three-note scale-to-triad cells
- A minor: A-B-C, then resolve to A-C-E.
- D minor: D-E-F, then resolve to D-F-A.
- E minor: E-F#-G, then resolve to E-G-B.
- C major: C-D-E, then resolve to C-E-G.
- G major: G-A-B, then resolve to G-B-D.

**Control rule:** Do not run the scale continuously during every solo. Use scale notes to travel between chord tones. The triad tells you where to arrive; the scale gives you a route.

## Module 4: Minor Triads and Scale Support

### How major becomes minor

Keep the root and fifth. Lower only the third by one fret. This single-note transformation should become automatic.

### C minor on D-G-B

| Inversion | D-G-B frets | Notes | Suggested fingering |
|---|---|---|---|
| Root | 10-8-8 | C-Eb-G | 4-1-1 |
| First | 13-12-13 | Eb-G-C | 2-1-3 |
| Second | 5-5-4 | G-C-Eb | 2-3-1 |

### C minor on A-D-G

| Inversion | A-D-G frets | Notes | Suggested fingering |
|---|---|---|---|
| Root | 3-1-0 | C-Eb-G | 3-1-open |
| First | 6-5-5 | Eb-G-C | 2-1-1 |
| Second | 10-10-8 | G-C-Eb | 2-3-1 |

### Minor chord exercises
- Major-to-minor switch: C-Cm, G-Gm, D-Dm. Change only the third.
- Minor inversion ladder: play Am in all three inversions up one string set and back down.
- Relative-pair drill: C-Am, G-Em, F-Dm. Identify common tones before moving.
- Minor i-iv-v: Am-Dm-Em using nearest inversions.
- Minor i-iv-V: Am-Dm-E. Hear how the major V creates stronger pull back to Am.

### Scale exercises that complement triads

| Exercise | Notes/formula | Application |
|---|---|---|
| A natural minor | A B C D E F G | Play slowly; stop on A-C-E to hear the Am triad. |
| A minor pentatonic | A C D E G | Connect your familiar box to Am chord tones. |
| C major | C D E F G A B | Same notes as A natural minor; change the tonal center. |
| A harmonic minor | A B C D E F G# | Use G# only when studying the E or E7 pull to Am. |
| Chord-tone landing | 1-3-5 of each chord | Improvise scale fragments but land on a triad tone when the chord changes. |

## Module 5: Smooth Voice Leading

### Five rules
1. Choose the nearest inversion, not automatically root position.
2. Keep common tones on the same string whenever possible.
3. Move each voice the shortest practical distance.
4. Practice the top voice separately as a melody.
5. Release pressure without lifting fingers far from the fretboard.

### Transition routine

| Pass | What to do | Tempo |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Place each chord silently; inspect all three notes. | No tempo |
| 2 | Four beats per chord; stop if any note buzzes. | 50-60 bpm |
| 3 | Two beats per chord with continuous eighth-note picking. | 60 bpm |
| 4 | One chord per beat; accent the top note. | 50-70 bpm |
| 5 | Play with recording; simplify rhythm before shapes. | Recording speed |

### Mechanical tips
- Look at the destination shape before you move.
- Identify a guide finger that stays or slides to the next chord.
- Prepare fingers above their destination during the final beat of the current chord.
- Practice the hardest two-chord seam repeatedly instead of restarting the full song.
- Use a phone recording to check timing, string noise, and whether the top voice sounds connected.

## Module 6: Song Laboratories

These are practice frameworks, not complete transcriptions. Recordings, live versions, and published arrangements may differ. Use licensed sheet music when you need the exact form.

### The Band: The Weight
- Primary goal: major I-IV-V awareness and lower-string triads.
- Start with A, D, and E triads on A-D-G.
- Round 1: one whole-note triad per chord change.
- Round 2: choose inversions that remain within a five-fret area.
- Round 3: alternate full triad and top two voices.

### Rolling Stones: Dead Flowers
- Primary goal: country-rock articulation on D-G-B.
- Use D, G, and A triads.
- Add short slides, partial strums, and rests rather than constant full strumming.
- Track the highest note and make it sound like a second guitar part.

### Van Morrison: Into the Mystic
- Primary goal: legato voice leading and sustained top notes.
- Map the song's chord chart from a licensed source, then choose the nearest D-G-B inversion for every change.
- Let common tones ring; avoid clipping each chord when the harmony changes.

### Grateful Dead: Franklin's Tower
- Primary goal: triad targeting over a repeating major-chord cycle.
- A common simplified practice cycle is A-G-D-G.
- First comp with triads; then improvise using only the notes of the current triad.
- Finally connect chord tones with one nearby scale note between changes.

## Module 7: More Song-Based Routines

### Grateful Dead: Ripple
- Primary goal: major-key chord family and chord melody preparation.
- Write the chord names from your licensed chart above a blank timeline.
- For each chord, circle the melody note if you can find it by ear.
- Select a triad inversion that places that note on the B or G string.
- Play the chord only at phrase landmarks, leaving space for the melody.

### Grateful Dead: Friend of the Devil
- Primary goal: major/minor recognition and quick position changes.
- Identify every major and minor chord in your chosen chart.
- Color-code major chords green and minor chords brown in the workbook.
- Practice only the minor-chord transitions first, then restore the full sequence.

### Grateful Dead: Brokedown Palace
- Primary goal: expressive major-key voice leading and chord melody.
- Map the harmony from a licensed chord chart, then keep each triad within one five-fret area.
- Sing or hum the top note of each voicing before playing it.
- Use sustained triads at phrase endings and leave space between vocal lines.

### Rolling Stones: Tumbling Dice
- Primary goal: blend major triads with minor-pentatonic fills.
- Play a triad stab, answer with a two- or three-note pentatonic phrase, then return to the next chord shape.
- End fills on a note belonging to the arriving triad.

## Module 8: Building Chord Melodies with Triads

### The method
1. Find the melody note by ear.
2. Identify the chord sounding under that note.
3. Locate an inversion with the melody note as the highest note.
4. If no plain triad fits, keep the melody and omit or adjust an inner voice.
5. Play the melody louder than the supporting notes.
6. Connect chord shapes with single notes, slides, or open strings.

### Original exercise in C major

Use this original four-bar top line. Each item shows chord / top note. Create your own rhythm.

| Beat group | Chord | Top note | Suggested D-G-B voicing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | C | E | 5-5-5 |
| 2 | F | F | 7-5-6 |
| 3 | G | G | 9-7-8 |
| 4 | C | G | 10-9-8 |

### Chord-melody practice with songs
- Brokedown Palace: begin by finding the vocal phrase-ending notes, then harmonize those landmarks.
- Ripple: harmonize one short phrase at a time; do not attempt the full song immediately.
- Into the Mystic: hold sustained melody notes while inner voices move underneath.
- The Weight: create an instrumental response by placing a simple top-line note over each major triad.

**Important:** A chord melody does not require a chord under every melody note. Strong arrangements alternate full triads, dyads, bass notes, and unaccompanied melody notes.

## Final Checkpoints

- [ ] I can name the four triad qualities.
- [ ] I can play three major inversions on D-G-B and A-D-G.
- [ ] I can turn a major triad into minor by lowering the third.
- [ ] I can play Am natural minor and identify A-C-E inside it.
- [ ] I can choose a nearby inversion instead of jumping to root position.
- [ ] I can hear and isolate the top voice of a triad progression.
- [ ] I can place a melody note above a supporting triad.
- [ ] I can follow at least two recordings using compact triads.
- [ ] I can improvise using the current triad tones before adding scale notes.

**Next step:** After these checkpoints feel comfortable, add the G-B-E string set, learn the remaining major-scale positions, and connect each scale position to the nearest major and minor triads. Do not add speed until the transitions sound connected and intentional.
