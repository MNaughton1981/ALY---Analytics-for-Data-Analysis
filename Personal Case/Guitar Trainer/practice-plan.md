# Your Practice Plan — 30 min × 3 days a week

A realistic structure for breaking the triads plateau on **90 minutes a week**. The triads course is the
core work; *Morning Coffee* / *Cream & Sugar* are used only as a short warm-up, played from your own copies.

> **Golden rules:** clean tone, metronome on, go slow. Say the chord name, inversion, and top note aloud.
> Never add speed until the transitions already sound connected.

---

## The weekly rhythm — 3 session types

Rotate three session "flavors" each week. Same 30-minute skeleton every time; only the middle 18 minutes change.

| Day | Flavor | What you're training |
|---|---|---|
| **Day 1 — SEE** | Shapes & inversions | Grab any triad inversion cleanly and name its top note. |
| **Day 2 — CONNECT** | Voice leading & neck maps | Move between chords using the *nearest* shape; link inversions up the neck. |
| **Day 3 — APPLY** | Music | Use triads in a real progression: chord melody, soloing, or a song lab. |

---

## The 30-minute session skeleton (use every session)

| Minutes | Block | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **0–5** | **Warm-up (the gym)** | From *Morning Coffee*: the **triad arpeggio** in this week's key, plus **one scale**, slow and relaxed. This is your bridge — the arpeggio is the *same three notes* as the chord shapes. To attack fretboard geography, spend some of this on **`scales-up-the-neck.html`**: a broken-interval line (3rds first) climbing up the neck, **reciting note + degree** for every note. |
| **5–23** | **Core focus (18 min)** | The Day-1 / Day-2 / Day-3 work from the week's row below. Loop the hardest two-chord *seam*, don't restart the whole thing. Run every loop with the **call-your-shot** habit below. |
| **23–28** | **Apply (5 min)** | Play the idea in time. Open **`practice-tools.html`** — turn on the metronome (or a bass-drum groove), or loop a **backing track** and comp/solo over it. Keep the tempo slow enough to stay clean. |
| **28–30** | **Log + 1 ear rep (2 min)** | One line in your log: *what improved*, *hardest seam*, *next target*. Then one quick round of the **Major/Minor ear trainer** in `practice-tools.html`. |

> **The one habit that makes it stick — "call your shot."** Don't just play the changes; *anticipate* them. One beat before every change: **(1) look** at the region the next shape lives in, **(2) say** its top note out loud, **(3) hum** that note — then land and check you were right. Saying it welds the fretboard region to the note name; humming it welds the note name to the sound; playing it confirms both. Just the **top note** is enough — it's your melody voice, and it's the highest-leverage thing to lock in.

**How the books plug in (≈5 min, own copies only):**
- Weeks 1–3 & 5–7: *Morning Coffee* — **major / minor triad arpeggios** and one scale in the week's key.
- Week 8 (qualities): *Cream & Sugar* — **diminished-7th and augmented arpeggios** to hear the tense qualities.
- **Broken intervals** (3rds, 4ths, 5ths, 7ths, octaves): once the boxed patterns feel familiar, take them up the *y-axis* with **`scales-up-the-neck.html`** — the same line climbing the neck on your triad string sets, reciting note + degree. This is the fastest route to knowing where every note lives (and to "third-hunting"). Start with **broken 3rds**. When you want Mixolydian, use the page's **bridge** shape (fill the skipped string with the 5th → `1-5-8`, then drop the top a whole step → `1-5-♭7`): on **G** that gives G-D-F, the key's own dominant.
- Skip the deeper rest (melodic minor, double-harmonic, 7th arpeggios) until triads feel solid — they're great, but off-topic for now.

## Ear training — a little, often

You don't need a separate ear-training practice. Fold tiny doses into what you're already doing — that's how it sticks without adding time or overwhelm.

- **The zero-cost habit:** before you play any chord or top note, **guess how it will sound**, then play it and check. You're already saying the note names aloud — now predict the sound too.
- **Sing the top voice.** Humming the melody note before you fret it (e.g., the D–E–D–E line in Week 1) trains your ear *and* your voice-leading at once.
- **2-minute drill:** the **Major/Minor ear trainer** in `practice-tools.html`. Two minutes at the end of a session is plenty. Add *diminished & augmented* only once major-vs-minor is easy (around Week 8).
- **Play-along listening:** when you loop a backing track, hum the root before each chord change so you *hear* the progression coming.

**Rough progression (no rush):**

| Phase | Ear goal | When |
|---|---|---|
| A | Major vs. minor triad, by sound | Weeks 1–4 |
| B | Hear the **top note** of a voicing; sing a progression's top line | Weeks 4–7 |
| C | Add diminished & augmented; recognize I–IV–V by ear | Week 8+ |
| D | Name a chord's **quality and root** from a recording | After the first cycle |

## The road ahead (realistic, layered)

One thing at a time. Each layer only gets added once the previous one feels comfortable — there is no deadline.

1. **Now → Week 8:** the 8-week cycle below. Core skill = *see the shape, choose the nearest inversion, hear the top note.*
2. **Cycle 2 (repeat in new keys):** run the same 8 weeks starting from A major / E minor. Same shapes, new fretboard locations — this is where they become automatic.
3. **Expansion pack (optional, borrowed from the Anyone Can Play Guitar course):** "triad scales" (all diatonic triads up one string set) and the **graduated soloing jams** — 1-chord → 2-chord → 3-chord → 4-chord, using the vamp/backing tracks in `practice-tools.html`.
4. **Later:** the low **E-A-D (strings 456)** set, full chord-melody arrangements, and adding colour tones (6ths, 7ths) to triads.

> If a layer ever feels like "too much," you've found the right place to stop for now. Stay one step behind your ambition and you'll keep making progress.

## 8-week cycle (24 sessions)

Do the three days in any order that fits your week. Diagrams for every shape below are in `index.html` / `triads-course.md`.
For the **Day-3 APPLY** block, `song-labs.html` has a printable page per song (*The Weight, Dead Flowers, Friend of the Devil,
Into the Mystic, Franklin's Tower, Ripple*) — each maps the week's chords to nearest-inversion triad voicings on D-G-B, the top-line
melody, and a song-specific drill.

| Week | Warm-up key(s) | Day 1 · SEE | Day 2 · CONNECT | Day 3 · APPLY |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | C, G major | C major, 3 inversions on **D-G-B** | G–C–D loop, nearest inversions (top voice **D-E-D-E**) | I-IV-V triads over *The Weight* feel |
| **2** | D, A major | D & A major shapes on **D-G-B** | D–G–A voice leading | *Dead Flowers* articulation (stabs, slides, rests) |
| **3** | C, G, D major | C major, 3 inversions on **A-D-G** | Connect **D-G-B ↔ A-D-G** (use the neck map) | *The Weight* on the A-D-G set |
| **4** | Am, Em, Dm | Major→minor flips (lower the 3rd) on both sets | Minor **i-iv-v**: Am–Dm–Em, nearest inversions | *Friend of the Devil*: spot & play the minor chords |
| **5** | key of the song | Review C & G shapes on both sets | Nearest-inversion **I-IV-V**; play the **top voice as a melody** | *Into the Mystic*: legato, let common tones ring |
| **6** | A, G, D major | Neck-map climb on **D-G-B** (all 3 inversions) | Neck-map climb on **A-D-G**, up and back down | *Franklin's Tower* cycle: improvise with **only** the current triad tones |
| **7** | C, F, G major | C major shapes on **G-B-E** | Melody-on-top voicings (Module 8: top line E-F-G-G) | Harmonize one phrase of *Ripple* / *Brokedown Palace* |
| **8** | (dim/aug) | All **four qualities** (add diminished & augmented) | Attack your **weakest seam** from the log — drill only that | *Tumbling Dice*: triad stab → pentatonic fill → next chord; run the **Final Checkpoints** |

**After Week 8:** repeat the cycle in fresh keys (e.g., E minor / A major), or push into full chord-melody arrangements. Do not advance a week until that week's Day-3 application feels musical, not mechanical.

---

## If you only get one session this week
Do **Day 2 (CONNECT)** in the key you last touched: 5-min arpeggio warm-up, then loop a I-IV-V with nearest inversions while singing the top note. It's the highest-leverage 30 minutes for the plateau.

## If you're short on time within a session
Cut the warm-up to 3 minutes and protect the core 18. The gym is optional; the triad work is not.
