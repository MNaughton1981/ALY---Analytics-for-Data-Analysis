Scales Up the Neck — Broken Intervals in C Major

The broken-interval studies (from your daily warm-up) are usually drawn as one boxed shape. These charts flip them onto the y-axis: the same C-major line climbing up the neck on the string sets you already know your triads on. Every note is labeled with its name and scale degree — say both out loud as you play. That recitation is the whole point: it welds the geography (where the note lives) to the theory (what the note is).

Why this matters — "third-hunting"

Jerry Garcia has been described as a third-hunter: as he solos through a tune, his ear reaches for the 3rd of the next chord, because the 3rd is the note that tells you major from minor and voice-leads most strongly into the change. Broken 3rds up the neck are the drill that builds exactly that reflex — you learn where every 3rd lives, in every register, on the string sets you comp on. Broken 4ths/5ths/7ths/octaves extend the same idea to the other target tones.

Play each line slowly, ascending then descending, reciting note + degree for every note. Then loop just the upper voice as a melody. You are not memorizing a shape — you're learning to see the note names climb the neck.
